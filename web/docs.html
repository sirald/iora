Iora documentation

Iora is a non-custodial vault on Stellar. Your assets earn interest on Blend while your limit orders wait, and when the price reaches your limit the order is filled from the vault in a single transaction.

Iora runs on Stellar testnet only. Everything on this page uses test funds. The contracts have not been audited.

Overview

A normal limit order leaves your money idle until the price comes to you. That can take days or weeks. Iora keeps the money at work in the meantime: whatever sits behind your order is supplied to a Blend lending pool and earns interest until the moment it is spent. You have one vault. It holds all of your assets, buys a coin at or below your price and sells it at or above your price, and what an order buys can stay in the vault and keep earning.

The name says it: Iora stands for Interest On Resting Assets.

Your own vault

Each user gets a separate smart contract. Only your wallet can deposit, withdraw, place or cancel orders.

Earning while waiting

The assets you deposit go to Blend right away, including the part reserved for open orders. What your orders buy can stay in the vault and keep earning too.

One-step fills

Withdraw from Blend, swap along the best route Iora's keeper finds among the venues it knows, and put the coin you bought back to work in your vault (or send it to your wallet), all in one atomic transaction.

Who it is for

  • People who want to buy a coin below today's price and don't want their cash idle while they wait.
  • Savers who buy in steps (dollar-cost averaging) and want each step to keep earning until it fills.
  • Wallets and DAOs on Stellar that want to offer yield-bearing limit orders without holding user funds.

Quick start

Getting set up on testnet takes about five minutes and costs nothing.

  1. Get an invite. The testnet app is invite-only during the beta. Ask for access with the beta form; you will receive an invite code, or your wallet is added to the access list. The invite only opens the app. It is not a security feature: the contracts are public on Stellar.
  2. Install a wallet. Get the Varial browser extension at varialwallet.com and create a wallet. Varial is the new name of Freighter: if you already have Freighter installed, it is the same wallet and works as it is. Never share your recovery phrase with anyone, including us.
  3. Switch to Testnet. In your wallet's settings, select the Testnet network.
  4. Connect. Open the testnet app, enter your invite code if asked, and press Connect wallet.
  5. Get test XLM. On the app's Test funds page, press Get 10,000 test XLM.
  6. Get test USDC. On the same page press Add USDC trustline, then Get 100 test USDC (once a day). You can also sell some XLM for USDC with a Market order on the Trade page (Sell side), or use Circle's faucet with Stellar Testnet.
  7. Open your vault. On the Trade page press Open my vault and sign. This deploys your personal vault contract. You do this once; the same vault is used for buying and for selling.
  8. Deposit. On the Portfolio page choose an asset, enter an amount and press Deposit. It starts earning on Blend immediately.
  9. Place an order. Fill in the order form on the Trade page, review and sign. Pick the pair at the top of the page. Switch the form to Sell to set how much of the coin to sell and the price you want for it; both use the same vault. Your open orders show up as dashed lines on the price chart. The chart shows trades in Iora's pool for the coin; before the pool's first trade it shows the coin's market price in USD as a dashed grey line (from Kraken, or the Reflector oracle), and a marker shows where the pool takes over.

Vaults

A vault is a smart contract deployed for one owner by the Iora factory. Its address is derived from your wallet address, so each wallet has exactly one vault and it can always be found again. One vault holds several assets: you deposit any of the supported assets, and you buy and sell from it. Whatever you deposit is supplied to Blend in the vault's name. Iora never pools user funds in a contract of its own, but every vault supplies to the same Blend pool (see Risks).

Each asset in the vault has its own figures, shown on the Portfolio page:

Balance on Blend
Your principal plus interest earned, read live from Blend.
Reserved for orders
The sum of what is still unfilled in your open orders that sell this asset. It keeps earning until it is spent. An order that spends one asset never ties up another.
Available
Balance minus reserved. This is what you can withdraw or use for a new order. While an order is open on an asset that is supplied to Blend, 0.001 of it is held back as a rounding buffer. Assets that are not on Blend have no buffer.
Interest earned
Balance minus principal. Principal is what you deposited plus what your orders bought and left in the vault, less what you withdrew or spent.

Depositing and withdrawing

Deposits move an asset from your wallet to Blend through the vault. Withdrawals move it from Blend back to your wallet and are limited to the available balance, so they can never take money that an open order needs. Leave the amount empty to withdraw everything available, interest included. To withdraw reserved money, cancel the order first. The app keeps at least 5 XLM in your wallet when you deposit XLM, so you can still pay network fees.

What happens to what you buy

When an order fills, the coin you bought stays in your vault by default. It is supplied to Blend in the same transaction and starts earning straight away, and you can use it for the next order or withdraw it whenever you like. If you untick Keep it in my vault on the order form, the coin is sent to your wallet instead. If your wallet cannot receive it (for example it has no trustline for that asset), the fill still goes through and the coin waits in your vault as part of your balance.

Buying and selling

The Trade page has two sides, Buy and Sell, and both work on the same vault. A buy order spends the USDC in your vault to buy a coin when 1 unit of it costs at most your price. A sell order sells the coin in your vault when 1 unit of it is worth at least your price. Either way the keeper makes the trade in one transaction when the price reaches yours, and until then the amount behind the order keeps earning on Blend.

Every rule on this page applies to both sides: only your wallet can move funds, the keeper can only fill an order when your price is met, and cancelling or withdrawing never depends on the keeper or on Iora (a withdrawal can still have to wait if the Blend pool is short on cash, see Risks).

You pay / You sell
The USDC (buy) or the coin (sell) set aside for the order. It is reserved from your available balance of that asset and keeps earning until it is spent.
Price
For a buy, the most USDC you pay for 1 unit of the coin. For a sell, the least USDC you accept for 1 unit. The order fills at this price or better, never worse.
You receive at least
Computed on-chain from your price and checked again after the swap.
Keep it in my vault
Ticked by default: what the order buys stays in your vault and earns. Unticked: it goes to your wallet.

Every market is a coin against USDC; the Markets page of the app lists them. Your assets earn interest while they wait, as long as the lending pool has a market for them. On testnet today that is USDC and XLM. The other coins are Iora's own testnet tokens, named “i” plus the coin they follow (iBTC, iETH, iSOL, iAVAX, iXRP). Iora issues them, they have no value, and their pools are kept at the real price of that coin from the Reflector oracle. Until the lending pool lists them they wait in your vault without earning (the app says “earns no interest yet”), and stop loss is not available for them.

Take profit and stop loss

Take profit (buy, then sell automatically). On the Buy side of the Trade page, tick Take profit and type a price before you place a buy order. When the buy fills, the coin stays in your vault (and keeps earning, if the lending pool lists it), and a sell order for it opens in the same transaction at your take profit price. That sell order stays open until it fills or you cancel it. There is nothing extra to sign. The take profit price cannot be below your buy price. If new orders are paused at that moment, the buy still fills and the coin simply waits in your vault as available balance.

Sell it higher. For a buy order that has already filled, the orders table shows a Sell higher button that places a sell order above what you paid.

Stop loss (coming with mainnet). You'll be able to set the price you don't want a coin to fall below. Your coin keeps earning on Blend. If its price drops to your stop price, the keeper sells it. The contract checks the price against the same price oracle Blend uses, not the swap pool, so nobody can trigger your stop by pushing the pool price for a moment. It never sells more than a band you choose (3% by default) below the oracle price: if the price gaps lower than that, the order waits instead of selling cheap. On testnet the oracle price is fixed, so stop loss is switched off in the app.

Limit orders

Iora supports two order directions from the same vault. A buy order buys a coin with USDC at or below a price you set, while the USDC earns on Blend. A sell order sells a coin for USDC at or above a price you set, while that coin earns on Blend. The rules below apply to both. The order form also offers Market (swap now, from your wallet) and Scaled (several limit orders spread between two prices).

Amount
The USDC (buy) or the coin (sell) set aside for the order. It is reserved from your available balance when you place the order.
Limit
For a buy order, the most USDC you are willing to pay for 1 unit of the coin. For a sell order, the least USDC you accept for 1 unit. The order fills at this price or better, never worse.
Minimum fill
The smallest piece the order may be filled in. Only the final remainder can be smaller. Left empty, the app uses a quarter of the amount.
Max price impact
How far a single fill may move the price of the pool it trades in (applies to the vault's built-in single-pool route). If the pool is thin, a large order is filled in smaller pieces instead. Default 3%.
Duration
Orders stay open until they fill or you cancel them; they have no end date.

Price improvement

The contract computes the minimum amount you must receive from your limit (the coin for a buy order, USDC for a sell order), and checks it again after the swap. Your limit is a guarantee; a better price is not. When the market is better than your limit at the moment of the fill, the swap can return more than the minimum. Whoever fills the order may take a small, capped fill fee out of that extra (see Keepers); the rest goes to you, and Iora itself takes no cut. That is how it works during the testnet beta; what Iora charges will be reviewed before mainnet. Filling is open to anyone, and whoever fills an order can arrange the trade so that it fills exactly at your limit and keep the difference themselves. You never receive less than your limit; you should not count on receiving more. To get a better price, set a better limit.

Partial fills

An order can fill in several pieces, for example when the pool cannot absorb the whole amount within your price impact limit. Each piece is a separate transaction and each one meets your limit on its own. The orders list on the Trade page shows how much has filled and your average price.

DCA (scheduled orders)

A DCA plan buys or sells a fixed amount at a fixed interval, for example 500 USDC of a coin every week. You find it on the DCA tab of the order form. It is an ordinary order with a schedule, and the schedule is enforced by your vault contract, not by the keeper: nobody can fill a part before it is due, and a fill is always exactly one part.

Amount each time
What one buy spends (in USDC) or one sale sells (in the coin).
Interval
The time between two parts, from 1 minute to 28 days. The first part is due right away.
Stop after
Optional. Left empty, the plan runs for as long as your vault can cover the next part.
Price limit
Optional. A ceiling when buying, a floor when selling. While the market is beyond it the plan waits; a part that was due fills as soon as the price allows, and later parts keep their interval, so missed parts do not fill in a burst.

Only the next part is set aside. The rest of your balance stays free: it keeps earning, and you can withdraw it or place other orders with it. When your vault cannot cover the next part, the plan ends by itself; depositing later does not restart it. You stop a plan at any time with Cancel in the order list.

A plan without a price limit is still protected: each part must be within 3% of the oracle price at that moment, otherwise it is skipped until the pool price is back in line. This keeps someone from moving a pool to fill your part at a bad price.

Order lifecycle

Open
Waiting for the price. The reserved amount keeps earning on Blend, if the pool lists that asset.
Partially filled
Still open; part of the amount has been swapped. What it bought is already in your vault (or in your wallet, if you chose that).
Filled
The whole amount has been spent.
Cancelled
You cancelled it. The unfilled part returns to your available balance immediately.

Where the yield comes from

Iora does not create yield. Your vault lends your assets on Blend, and Blend borrowers pay interest on what they borrow. Each asset has its own rate. Lenders share that interest: the supply rate is roughly the borrow rate multiplied by the pool's utilization, so it rises when borrowing demand is high and falls when it is low. Interest is added to your balance continuously and paid out when you withdraw or when an order spends it.

  • The rate changes all the time; past rates are not a promise.
  • BLND token emissions that some Blend pools pay to lenders are not claimed automatically. The vault owner can claim them to their own wallet with claim_rewards; the app has no button for it yet.
  • The landing page simulation is sped up for illustration and does not show real rates.

Keepers

Orders are filled by a keeper, a bot that watches the price. Filling is permissionless: the execute function can be called by anyone, so anyone can run a keeper. The caller only chooses which order to fill, how much, and the address that receives the fill fee. The tokens, the recipient, the limit and the minimum output are fixed by the contract.

  • The Iora keeper scans all vaults on every new Stellar ledger (about every 5 seconds) and quotes each open order, in its own direction, across the trading venues it knows.
  • When your limit is met, it tries the full remaining amount. If the price impact is too high, it halves the piece, down to your minimum fill.
  • The keeper pays the network fee for fills. In return, whoever fills an order receives a small fill fee: a small capped amount per fill, paid in the coin the order buys (on testnet: about 0.003 USDC, or the same value in the coin bought). The fee is taken only from the part of a fill that came out better than your limit, so you always receive at least what your limit promises. If a fill lands exactly on your limit, the fee is zero. These amounts apply during the testnet beta; the fill fee will be reviewed before mainnet.
  • Because the fee has to come out of that difference, the keeper waits until the price has passed your limit by enough to cover it. For most orders this is a tiny fraction of a percent. Very small orders are allowed, but they need a larger move past the limit before they fill.
  • If the keeper is offline, orders simply wait. Your funds are unaffected, and cancelling and withdrawing do not need the keeper.

Where an order fills

Iora is its own router: for every fill the keeper compares the pools of all the trading venues it knows (on testnet: Aquarius and Soroswap) and takes the route that returns the most. That can be one pool, the amount split over several pools, or two steps through an intermediate coin. No venue is preferred. Open orders of two Iora users that meet each other's price can also be filled against each other without any pool. Whichever route is used, your vault hands over only the amount being filled, counts what comes back, and undoes the whole transaction if that is less than your limit promises. The route is the keeper's choice; the limit is the contract's guarantee.

Lend & Borrow

The app also has a Lend & Borrow page for using Blend directly, without a limit order. These positions are opened from your own wallet on the same Blend pool; Iora's vault contracts are not involved, and your vault and its orders are not affected.

Supply
Deposits an asset as collateral. It earns the supply rate and raises your borrowing power.
Withdraw
Takes collateral back, as long as what remains still covers your debt.
Borrow
Sends the asset to your wallet. Interest is added to your debt over time.
Repay
Pays debt back. Paying more than you owe is safe; Blend returns the extra in the same transaction.
Health factor
Your collateral times each asset's collateral factor, divided by your debt divided by each asset's liability factor, all valued with the pool's oracle prices. Below 1, part of your collateral can be liquidated.

Borrowing carries liquidation risk. Keep your health factor well above 1, especially when borrowing an asset whose price moves differently from your collateral.

What the contract guarantees

  • Deposits, withdrawals, orders and cancellations require the vault owner's signature.
  • No key can move your funds and there is no upgrade function. Nobody can change a deployed vault's code. The factory's guardian can only pause new vaults and new orders and edit the list of coins that new orders may use; withdrawals, cancellations and fills keep working.
  • What a fill buys always goes to the vault owner: it stays in the owner's vault or is sent to the owner's wallet, as the owner chose when placing the order. A keeper cannot redirect it. The vault itself pays a filler at most the capped fill fee, and only out of output above your limit.
  • The contract guarantees your signed limit, not a better price. Whoever fills an order can arrange the trade so that it fills exactly at your limit and keep any price improvement beyond it. Passing price improvement on to you is how Iora's keeper behaves, not something the contract guarantees (see Price improvement).
  • The minimum output is computed on-chain from your limit and checked again after the swap. If it is not met, the whole transaction reverts and your funds stay on Blend.
  • The same balance cannot back two orders, or an order and a withdrawal.
  • Swaps have a five-minute deadline, so a delayed transaction cannot execute at a stale price.

Risks

Using Iora means trusting its contracts and the protocols it is built on. Please read these before depositing.

Blend pool risk
For now, your deposit sits in a Blend lending pool that Iora does not run. The pool, its price oracle and its backstop are run by Blend and the pool's operators. Iora does not control them and cannot make up a loss that happens there. Once there is enough liquidity, we plan to open Iora's own lending pools; that would give Iora the pool's risk to manage, not remove it. Until then every vault uses this Blend pool. All vaults supply to the same Blend pool. If a borrower's debt exceeds their collateral, for example after an oracle failure, the loss is covered first by Blend's backstop. If the backstop cannot cover it, the remaining bad debt is spread across lenders, which lowers the value of every deposit in that pool, including your vault's.
Liquidity risk
When most of the pool is borrowed, there may not be enough cash to withdraw or fill right away. High utilization pushes rates up, which usually brings cash back quickly.
Smart contract risk
All vaults run the same code, so a bug could affect every vault. The contracts have not yet been independently audited.
Swap and price risk
Fills go through a trading venue's pool for the two tokens of the order (whichever venue pays the most at that moment), so a bug in a venue is also a risk; the most a single fill exposes is the amount being filled. Your limit protects the price you pay, but a thin or manipulated pool can delay fills, and whoever fills your order can keep any price improvement beyond your limit (see Price improvement).
Oracle risk (stop loss)
Stop loss orders rely on the price oracle of the Blend pool. If the oracle stops updating or reports a wrong price, a stop can fail to trigger, trigger late, or wait while the price falls quickly. A stop loss limits losses in normal markets; it is not a guarantee.
Keeper risk
If no keeper is running, orders do not fill, and you can miss a price that came and went. Today Iora runs a single keeper, so it can be offline for hours. This never puts funds at risk: cancelling and withdrawing do not need the keeper.
Coins that can be traded
A vault only accepts orders between coins on the factory's allowed list. An order that buys any other token is rejected by the contract, so nobody can trick you into signing an order for a fake token. The guardian can add a coin to the list or remove one; removing only blocks new orders, and you can always withdraw whatever your vault holds.
Sending to a wallet without a trustline
If you choose to have a fill sent to your wallet and the wallet cannot hold that asset (for example no USDC trustline), the fill still happens and the coin stays in your vault instead.
Website and network access
This website and the public RPC servers it uses can be slow or unavailable. Your funds do not depend on them: the vault is a contract on Stellar, and its owner can call cancel_order and withdraw with any Soroban tool (for example the Stellar CLI or Stellar Lab) using the vault address shown in the app. Write that address down.
Phishing
Always check the address bar (iorafi.com) and read what your wallet asks you to sign. Iora will never ask for your recovery phrase.

Emergency pause

The factory has a guardian address that can pause the system and edit the list of coins that orders may use. Pausing stops new vaults and new orders only. Withdrawals, cancellations and fills of existing orders keep working, and the guardian has no way to move user funds.

Audits

The contracts have not been audited yet. A written threat model is kept in the repository. Iora will not launch on mainnet before an independent security audit is complete, and the report will be published here.

FAQ

Can I sell coins too, not just buy?

Yes. The same vault holds all of your assets. Pick a pair, and on the Sell side of the Trade page set the price at which your coin is sold for USDC; it keeps earning on Blend until then. More pairs will follow.

I got test USDC from another faucet. Why can't I use it?

On Stellar a token is identified by its name and its issuer. On testnet Iora uses Circle's test USDC (issuer GBBD47IF…FLA5), so USDC from Circle's faucet works here. Test USDC from other sources, such as older Blend or Soroswap test tokens, has the same name but a different issuer, so it is a different token and will not show up here. Get the right one on the Test funds page. On mainnet there is only one USDC.

Does Iora hold my funds?

No. Your funds are in your own vault contract and, through it, on Blend. Neither Iora nor the keeper can withdraw them.

Why not use Stellar's built-in order book?

An order on the Stellar order book keeps your funds idle until it fills. With Iora, the amount waiting for your price earns interest on Blend.

Why hasn't my order filled?

The price may not have passed your limit by enough to cover the fill fee, the lending pool may be short of cash at that moment, or the pool the fill trades in may be too thin for your price impact limit. The orders list on the Trade page shows the status.

Can I lose money?

Yes. Blend bad debt, a contract bug or a phishing site could cause losses. See Risks. On testnet only test funds are used.

How much interest will I earn?

It depends on how much of the Blend pool is borrowed, and changes constantly. The Portfolio page shows what you have earned.

When will Iora launch on mainnet?

Not before an independent security audit is complete.

Glossary

Blend
A lending protocol on Stellar. Your vault supplies your assets to it to earn interest.
Backstop
Blend's insurance layer that absorbs bad debt before lenders do.
Fill
Spending part or all of an order's amount: USDC to buy a coin, or a coin sold for USDC.
Friendbot
A testnet service that sends free test XLM.
Guardian
The address that can pause new vaults and orders and edit the list of tradable coins. It cannot move funds.
Keeper
A bot that watches prices and calls execute when an order can fill.
Vault
Your own contract. It holds your assets, supplies them to Blend, and places buy and sell orders from them.
Limit price
For a buy order, the most USDC you will pay for 1 unit of a coin; for a sell order, the least USDC you accept for 1 unit.
Buy vault, sell vault
The two separate vaults used by earlier versions. They still work for the people who opened them.
Price impact
How much a trade moves a pool's price; larger trades in smaller pools move it more.
Trading venues
The decentralized exchanges on Stellar where the swaps happen (for example Aquarius and Soroswap). Iora picks the route that pays the most for each fill.
Trustline
A one-time Stellar setting that lets your account hold an asset such as USDC.
Utilization
The share of a Blend pool that is currently borrowed.

How it works

Four steps on Stellar, each one signed by you in your own wallet.

  1. Open your vault

    One signature deploys a vault contract that belongs only to you. Your funds are never mixed with other users' in an Iora contract.

  2. Deposit

    Put stablecoins, the coin you want to sell, or both into the same vault. The deposit goes straight to Blend and starts earning. Whatever is not reserved for an order can be withdrawn with its interest; only you can do it.

  3. Set your price

    Choose the price you want to buy or sell at. The amount set aside for the order keeps earning while it waits.

  4. It fills at your price

    When your limit is met, the vault withdraws from Blend and swaps on Soroswap in one transaction. What you bought stays in your vault and keeps earning, or goes to your wallet if you prefer.

Built for patient buyers and sellers

Buy the dip

Set a price below the market, in one order or in steps. Your money earns until it fills; if the price never gets there, you still kept the interest.

Sell the rally

Set a price above the market for a coin you plan to sell. It earns interest in that coin until your price comes, then it is sold.

Wallets and DAOs

Offer yield-bearing limit orders to your users or treasury. Users sign in their own wallet; Iora never holds keys.

You stay in control

Trust comes from what the contract allows, not from promises. These rules are enforced on-chain.

Read the security docs
No one can withdraw for you
Withdrawals, orders and cancellations require your wallet signature. No key can move your funds and there is no upgrade function.
Never worse than your limit
The minimum amount you receive is computed on-chain and checked after the swap. If it isn't met, the whole transaction reverts and your funds stay on Blend.
The keeper can only trigger
The bot that fills orders cannot change the recipient, the amount or the limit. What you buy, or what your sale brings in, always goes to the vault owner: into the vault or to the owner's wallet.
Pausing only stops new orders
The Iora team holds an emergency switch. It stops new vaults and new orders and can change which coins may be traded. It cannot block withdrawals, cancellations or fills, and it cannot move funds. The team also runs the keeper today; if the keeper is offline, orders wait and your funds stay withdrawable.

Risks you should know

  • All vaults supply to the same Blend pool. If that pool takes bad debt that its backstop cannot cover, every deposit in it loses value.
  • The contracts have not been independently audited yet. Iora runs on testnet only, with test funds.
  • If the pool is short on cash, a withdrawal or fill may have to wait.

Questions

What does Iora mean?

It stands for Interest On Resting Assets. Money that waits behind a limit order is resting; with Iora it earns interest on Blend until the order fills.

Does Iora hold my funds?

No. Your funds are in your own vault contract and, through it, on Blend. Neither Iora nor the keeper can withdraw them.

Where does the interest come from?

Blend borrowers pay interest on what they borrow, and lenders share it. Your vault is a lender. The rate depends on how much of the pool is borrowed.

What if the price never reaches my limit?

The order simply expires or you cancel it. Your deposit kept earning the whole time and you withdraw it with the interest. The usual Blend pool risks apply while it is deposited.

Which coins can I trade?

Every market is a coin against USDC. On testnet today that is XLM, plus four Soroswap test tokens: BTC, EURC, XRP and XTAR. Your assets earn interest on Blend while they wait, as long as the pool has a market for them; the testnet pool does not list the four test tokens yet, so those wait in your vault without earning. More pairs come next, and later coins on other networks.

Can I lose money?

Yes. Bad debt in the Blend pool, a contract bug or a phishing site could cause losses. The risks page explains each one.

When is mainnet?

Not before an independent security audit is complete.
